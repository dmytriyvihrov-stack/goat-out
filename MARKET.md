# GOAT OUT — market

Research done 12–14 Sep 2026. What the comparable games actually earned, what the 2026 storefront
looks like, and which of the two viable strategies this project is on. `CONCEPT.md` is what the game
is; this file is who would buy it and why.

Nothing here is a design decision. It is the evidence a design decision should be made against, and
the open questions at the end are the decisions that have not been made.

---

## 1. The reference class earns less than its reputation

Goat Out's mechanical parents are critically beloved and commercially mid. This is the single most
important number in the file.

| Game | Steam reviews | Owners (SteamSpy) | Price | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ape Out** (2019) | 2 528 · 94% | 200–500k *(inflated by bundles)* | $14.99 | Devolver, Metacritic 83 |
| **Anger Foot** (2024) | 2 169 · 93% | 50–100k | €19.99 | Devolver + Free Lives, huge festival hype |
| **Bloodroots** (2020) | ~200 · 89% | 20–50k | $19.99 | Metacritic 76, strong press |
| **Kill Knight** (2024) | — | 100–200k | $14.99 | PlaySide, arcade top-down |

Ape Out at 2 528 reviews over seven years is roughly 80–130k real Steam sales on the standard
review-multiplier estimate — on the order of $0.7–1.2M gross lifetime, with Devolver's marketing
behind it and a cult reputation. Switch was Devolver's strongest console and adds to that, but not by
an order of magnitude.

**Read this as a ceiling, not a floor.** A perfectly executed Ape Out-like, published well, lands
somewhere between 20k and 130k copies. That is the genre's gravity. Beating it requires something
that is not the genre.

---

## 2. What clears that ceiling

| Game | Result | What carried it |
|---|---|---|
| **Untitled Goose Game** (2019) | 1M in 3 months, GOTY at DICE and GDCA | One silhouette. No dialogue, no cast, no story. The goose became an internet character. |
| **Cult of the Lamb** (2022) | 1M in week one, 4.5M by Oct 2024 | Cute-over-cruel contrast, instantly legible from one image. |
| **Balatro** (2024) | 5M in under a year; $9.3M net on mobile alone; TGA Best Indie | Solo dev. Zero characters, zero story, no art budget. Pure combinatorial depth. |
| **Goat Simulator** (2014) | 90%+ of 30k+ reviews, three-game franchise | One joke about a goat as a physics object. |

Two different escapes from the ceiling, and they are not the same escape:

- **The Goose route.** A protagonist legible in a single frame does the marketing for free. Cost:
  near zero — it is a silhouette and a framing decision, not an art budget.
- **The Balatro route.** Mechanical depth that keeps producing surprise for a hundred hours. Cost:
  high, but paid in systems and content rather than in art and writing.

Both are open to this project. They are not in conflict — see §4.

---

## 3. The Hades objection, and why it points the other way

The obvious counter is "mechanics can top the charts — look at Hades." Hades is the wrong banner for
that argument, because it is the most character-expensive indie game of its generation:

- Supergiant was ~20 people during development, in their sixth year as a studio, with a dedicated
  full-time writer (creative director Greg Kasavin).
- Nearly two years in Early Access before the September 2020 release.
- ~10 hours of chained dialogue, a fully voiced ensemble cast.
- It won the **Hugo Award for Best Video Game** (the first game ever to win one), the **Nebula Award
  for Best Game Writing**, and the **BAFTA for Narrative**. Three of its four headline awards are
  writing awards.

Hades' combat is excellent, but what turned it from a good roguelite into a phenomenon is that every
death advanced a relationship. Citing it as proof that characters are optional inverts the actual
case. Notably, it sold ~1M by three days after full release — strong, but in the same order as Goose
Game, which was made by four people with no writing at all.

**The right banner for "no resource for characters" is Balatro.** One developer, no characters, no
narrative, 5M copies. That argument is sound. It just needs a different example, and — importantly —
a different set of obligations, listed next.

---

## 4. The two departments are not the same department

The recommendation that came out of this research was misread as "invest in characters." It was not.
It is this:

> **Build mechanics-first. Market goat-first.**

These cost different things and are decided at different times.

**Mechanics-first (the build).** Already the project's direction and already substantially realised:
six verbs, boons that bend them, five levels, five enemy kinds, geometry kills. Keep going. This is
where the hours go.

**Goat-first (the store page).** Costs no development hours at all. It is a decision about what
occupies the first three seconds of the trailer, the capsule image, and the first sentence of the
description. The goat already exists, already has a readable silhouette, already screams, already
takes blood onto its coat. Goose Game shipped with one white shape and no dialogue and that was
enough. The failure mode to avoid is a store page that leads with the systems — that page reads as
"Ape Out clone" and inherits the §1 ceiling.

Nothing in "goat-first" requires a writer, a voice cast, a named NPC, or a line of dialogue.

## 5. What the Balatro route actually obliges

If mechanical depth is the bet, it is not the cheap option — it is differently expensive. The
obligations, in order of how badly they bite:

1. **Surprise has to survive past hour ten.** Balatro's jokers combine combinatorially; Brotato's
   items do; Vampire Survivors' evolutions do. Goat Out's boons are the right shape, and the rule
   that they bend existing verbs rather than add buttons is exactly the constraint that makes
   combinations legible. The question is whether there are enough of them, and whether they
   interact rather than stack.
2. **A run has to be legible as a build.** "This run I'm the Dragon Breath goat" needs to be a thing
   a player can say. That identity comes from mechanics, not art, and it is what makes a run worth
   describing to someone else.
3. **Something has to be screenshot-worthy and produced by play.** Balatro went viral on players
   posting absurd score screenshots. Goat Out's native equivalent already exists in the design: the
   persistent decal canvas means a finished level *is* a painting of the run. That is a marketing
   asset generated by the systems — no artist involved. Raising photo mode and GIF export out of
   LATER is the single highest-leverage marketing work available to this project, and it is
   engineering work.
4. **The content threshold is real.** The depth route pays only above a certain volume of
   interacting content. Below it, the outcome is Bloodroots: excellent, praised, 20–50k. This is the
   main cost and the main risk of the route.

## 6. The 2026 storefront

- **20 282 games** shipped on Steam in 2025. **608** (3%) reached 1 000 reviews.
- Median indie lifetime revenue is **$5–15k**. The top 1% take ~90% of revenue.
- **TikTok organic reach has collapsed** for game marketing: devlog formats that did 800k views in
  2024 now average 300–800. Short-form is a signal channel, not a discovery channel. Wishlists and
  streamers are what Steam's algorithm responds to.
- **Price.** Arcade breakouts frequently sit under $5. Ape Out's $14.99 is not the right anchor for
  a game of 1.5–4 minute runs in 2026; $7.99–9.99 is more defensible. Balatro shipped at $14.99, but
  with hundreds of hours of depth behind it — the price follows the route chosen in §4/§5.
- **Devolver's pitch form is open**, currently taking pitches for 2027 and later. They published both
  Ape Out and Cult of the Lamb; this project sits exactly on that intersection. A pitch needs a build
  with its own visual identity — canvas-primitive placeholders will not carry it.

## 7. Distribution

**Steam is the product.** Everything else is a funnel into it.

**Mobile is not the product.** 96% of mobile downloads are free-to-play. The entire premium mobile
segment did ~$10.2M across ~750 titles in the first five months of 2026 — roughly $13k per title. A
one-life precision arcade game at a premium price is close to the worst position available in that
store. The touch controls are still worth having: they make the browser build playable on a phone,
which is a funnel property, not a revenue plan. (Balatro's $9.3M mobile net is the exception that
proves it: it arrived on mobile *after* being a PC phenomenon.)

**The browser build is the funnel.** A free three-level web build plus itch.io is how this genre
gets tried. The portals are a separate question: Poki (60M MAU) and CrazyGames (35M MAU) pay
roughly 50–80% ad revenue share, with non-exclusive licences around $300–800 and exclusives from
$5 000 — but their audience is casual and largely young, and the blood almost certainly disqualifies
the game as it stands. Treat portals as unavailable unless §8's tone question is reopened.

## 8. Risks

1. **Derivative read.** From a store page, this is currently legible as "Ape Out with a goat." The
   defences are the things Ape Out does not have: the scream as an offensive tool, boons, the mill,
   the wraith. They are in the build; they need to be in the pitch.
2. **Blood costs more than it looks like.** Goose Game works partly because there is nothing to age-
   gate: streamers, press, ads and portals all take it without friction. Deadpan comedy plus folk
   horror plus gore is three tones at once, and the gore cuts exactly the audience that spreads
   things. This is a commercial fork, not a nitpick — a version where cultists are comically flung
   rather than dismembered has a materially larger addressable audience. Worth deciding deliberately
   rather than by default.
3. **Audience mismatch.** One life, procedural generation and committed animations select for a
   hardcore audience. A goat fleeing a cult sells to a much broader one. Marketing to the second and
   shipping to the first is the classic way a game gets refunded. This is what a difficulty or
   assist option is actually for, and it is a revenue decision.
4. **The meme does not travel.** "Будут ли жертвы" is Russian-language. It lands in CIS and means
   nothing in English, and CIS is the weakest large Steam region by revenue (recommended prices
   40–60% below USD, plus payment friction since 2022). The opening scene already plays the premise
   out without naming it, which is the right call. Do not build marketing on the meme.
5. **Scope.** The brief's Acts 3 and 4 are how a six-month prototype becomes a three-year project
   that lands at 20–50k copies. Bloodroots' lesson is short bursts, not endurance.

---

## 9. Open decisions

These are unresolved and belong to the user, not to a session:

- **Route.** Depth (Balatro) or brevity (Goose). The build is currently on the depth route. If that
  is deliberate, §5's four obligations become the roadmap and the price goes up, not down.
- **Gore.** Keep, or dial to comic. Decides portals, streamer reach, age gating and roughly who the
  game is for.
- **Price.** $7.99–9.99 for a short arcade game, or $14.99 justified by depth. Follows the route.
- **Photo mode / GIF export.** Currently LATER. On the evidence it is the highest-leverage
  marketing work available, and it is engineering, not art.
- **Publisher.** Pitch Devolver after a build with its own visual identity, or self-publish.
